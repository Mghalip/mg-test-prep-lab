📝 Warm-up · 例题★★★★Warm-up

Teens vs. adults online 青少年与成年人上网

A random sample of 800 U.S. teens and a separate random sample of 2253 U.S. adults were asked if they use social-networking sites. 73% of teens and 47% of adults said yes. 两个独立随机样本。

Q1Give a point estimate for the difference p_{T} − p_{A}.
p̂_{T} − p̂_{A} = 0.73 − 0.47 = 0.26.
Q2Would another pair of samples give exactly the same difference? What do we need to say how far off it might be?
Step 1. No — sampling variability. We need the standard deviation of p̂₁ − p̂₂ (from 3.9): √(p₁(1 − p₁)/n₁ + p₂(1 − p₂)/n₂).
Step 2. Estimate it with p̂’s (standard error), then use point estimate ± margin of error → a confidence interval for p_{T} − p_{A}.
🔑 Statistic: p̂_{T} − p̂_{A}.
💡 Same idea as Lesson 4 — just a new statistic and a new standard error.

Difficulty 难度 ★★★★

Review: Sampling Distribution of p̂₁ − p̂₂ 复习：p̂₁ − p̂₂ 的抽样分布

Center中心
μ_{p̂₁−p̂₂} = p₁ − p₂ — p̂₁ − p̂₂ is an unbiased estimator of p₁ − p₂.
均值 = p₁ − p₂（无偏估计）。
Variability离散程度
σ_{p̂₁−p̂₂} = √(p₁(1 − p₁)/n₁ + p₂(1 − p₂)/n₂) when the samples are independent (and each n ≤ 10% of its population). Variances add, not SDs.
两样本独立时，标准差 = √(p₁(1−p₁)/n₁ + p₂(1−p₂)/n₂)。方差相加，不是标准差相加。
Shape形状
Approximately normal if n₁p₁, n₁(1 − p₁), n₂p₂, n₂(1 − p₂) are all ≥ 10.
四个计数都 ≥ 10 时近似正态。

Conditions for a Two-Sample z Interval 两样本 z 区间的条件

Random随机
Data come from two independent random samples or from two groups in a randomized experiment.
数据来自两个独立随机样本或随机化实验中的两组。
10%10% 条件
When sampling without replacement: n₁ ≤ 0.10N₁ and n₂ ≤ 0.10N₂. (Not needed for an experiment with random assignment.)
不放回抽样：n₁ ≤ 0.10N₁ 且 n₂ ≤ 0.10N₂（随机分配的实验不需要）。
Large Counts大计数条件
n₁p̂₁, n₁(1 − p̂₁), n₂p̂₂, n₂(1 − p̂₂) are all at least 10 — the observed successes and failures in each group.
四个数 n₁p̂₁、n₁(1−p̂₁)、n₂p̂₂、n₂(1−p̂₂) 都 ≥ 10（每组的成功与失败数）。

3.10 · Key ConceptsEK 3.10.C–D
Difficulty 难度 ★★★★

The Two-Sample z Interval for p₁ − p₂ 两样本 z 区间

Formula公式
(p̂₁ − p̂₂) ± z* · √(p̂₁(1 − p̂₁)/n₁ + p̂₂(1 − p̂₂)/n₂) — statistic ± (critical value)(standard error). z* comes from the confidence level (1.645 for 90%, 1.96 for 95%, 2.576 for 99%).
(p̂₁ − p̂₂) ± z*·SE；z* 由置信水平决定（90%: 1.645，95%: 1.96，99%: 2.576）。
Order matters顺序
Define which population is “1”. Reversing the order flips the signs: (a, b) becomes (−b, −a).
先定义谁是 1。顺序反过来，区间变成 (−b, −a)。
(\hat p_1-\hat p_2)\pm z^*\sqrt{\frac{\hat p_1(1-\hat p_1)}{n_1}+\frac{\hat p_2(1-\hat p_2)}{n_2}}

📝 Example 2 · 例题★★★★EK 3.10 · 3.11

Teens vs. adults: the interval 青少年与成年人：构造区间

Random samples: 800 teens (73% use social networks) and 2253 adults (47%).

Construct and interpret a 95% confidence interval for p_{T} − p_{A}. Follow the four-step process.
Step 1. State: 95% CI for p_{T} − p_{A}, where p_{T} = proportion of all U.S. teens and p_{A} = proportion of all U.S. adults who use social-networking sites.
Step 2. Plan: two-sample z interval for p₁ − p₂. Two independent random samples ✓; 800 < 10% of all teens, 2253 < 10% of all adults ✓; 800(0.73) = 584, 800(0.27) = 216, 2253(0.47) ≈ 1059, 2253(0.53) ≈ 1194 ≥ 10 ✓.
Step 3. Do: SE = √(0.73·0.27/800 + 0.47·0.53/2253) ≈ 0.0189; 0.26 ± 1.96(0.0189) = 0.26 ± 0.037 → (0.223, 0.297).
Step 4. Conclude: We are 95% confident that the interval from 0.223 to 0.297 captures the true difference in the proportions of U.S. teens and adults who use social-networking sites. All values are positive → teens are 22.3 to 29.7 percentage points more likely.
🔑 State → Plan → Do → Conclude.

Interpreting the Interval and Justifying Claims 解释区间与支持结论

Interval & confidence level区间与置信水平
“We are C% confident that the interval from a to b captures the true difference in the proportions of (context 1) and (context 2) who (success).” C% = in many pairs of random samples, about C% of the intervals capture the true p₁ − p₂.
“我们有 C% 的信心，(a, b) 包含两总体比例的真实差值。” C% 指重复抽样时约 C% 的区间包含真实差值。
Is 0 in it?0 在区间内吗？
0 inside → “no difference” is plausible; not convincing evidence of a difference. Entire interval > 0 → convincing evidence p₁ > p₂. Entire interval < 0 → convincing evidence p₁ < p₂.
0 在区间内 → 没有差异是合理的；整个区间 > 0 → 有充分证据 p₁ > p₂；整个区间 < 0 → p₁ < p₂。

📝 Example 3 · 例题★★★★EK 3.11

Hearing loss in teens 青少年听力损失

Two national random samples of teens: in 1988–1994, 14.9% of 2928 teens had some hearing loss; in 2005–2006, 19.5% of 1771 teens did. A 95% CI for p_{2005} − p_{1988} is (0.023, 0.069).

aInterpret the interval.
We are 95% confident that the interval from 0.023 to 0.069 captures the true difference in the proportions of all U.S. teens with some hearing loss (2005–06 minus 1988–94).
bInterpret the confidence level.
If we took many pairs of random samples like these and built a 95% interval from each pair, about 95% of the intervals would capture the true difference.
cDoes the interval give convincing evidence that hearing loss became more common? Explain.
Yes. The entire interval is above 0 (0 is not a plausible value) → convincing evidence that the proportion of teens with hearing loss increased, by about 2.3 to 6.9 percentage points.
🔑 Look at the sign of every value in the interval.

📝 Example 4 · 例题★★★★EK 3.11

Breakfast at two schools 两校早餐

Random samples of 150 students at School A and 150 students at School B: 81 at A and 69 at B eat breakfast every day. A 95% CI for p_{A} − p_{B} is (−0.033, 0.193).

aCheck the conditions.
Random: two independent random samples ✓. 10%: 150 < 10% of each school (assume each school has at least 1500 students) ✓. Large Counts: 81, 69, 69, 81 all ≥ 10 ✓.
bA newspaper says “students at School A are more likely to eat breakfast.” Does the interval support this claim?
Step 1. The interval contains 0 (and negative values), so “no difference” is plausible. The data do not give convincing evidence that School A students are more likely to eat breakfast — even though p̂_{A} = 0.54 > p̂_{B} = 0.46.
Step 2. Careful: this does not prove the proportions are equal. It only says a difference of 0 is plausible.
🔑 0 in the interval → no convincing evidence of a difference.

📝 Example 5 · 例题★★★★EK 3.10 · 3.11

Flashcards vs. practice tests 闪卡与模拟测验

A teacher randomly assigns 240 volunteer students to study with practice tests (120) or flashcards (120). On the unit test, 93 of the practice-test group and 78 of the flashcard group pass.

aConstruct a 95% confidence interval for p_{T} − p_{F}.
Step 1. Conditions: random assignment ✓ (10% not needed); 93, 27, 78, 42 ≥ 10 ✓.
Step 2. p̂_{T} = 93/120 = 0.775, p̂_{F} = 78/120 = 0.65; SE = √(0.775·0.225/120 + 0.65·0.35/120) ≈ 0.0579; 0.125 ± 1.96(0.0579) → (0.012, 0.238).
bWhat does the interval suggest? Can we conclude that the study method causes a difference?
Step 1. The whole interval is above 0 → convincing evidence that practice tests lead to a higher pass rate, by about 1.2 to 23.8 points.
Step 2. Because the treatments were randomly assigned, we can conclude that the method causes the difference — but only for students like these volunteers.
🔑 Randomized experiment → random assignment replaces random sampling.

Which standard error?

Random samples of 200 men and 250 women: 56% of men and 62% of women prefer online shopping. Which expression is the standard error for a confidence interval for p_{M} − p_{W}?
  • (A) √(0.56·0.44/200) + √(0.62·0.38/250)
  • (B) √(0.56·0.44/200 + 0.62·0.38/250)
  • (C) √(0.59·0.41(1/200 + 1/250))
  • (D) √(0.56·0.44 + 0.62·0.38)/450
  • (E) (0.56·0.44/200) + (0.62·0.38/250)

SE = √(p̂₁(1 − p̂₁)/n₁ + p̂₂(1 − p̂₂)/n₂) → (B).

(A) adds SDs; (C) uses a pooled p̂ (that is for tests in Lesson 8); (E) forgets the square root.

📝 AP Practice 7 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 3.11

Interval containing 0

A 95% confidence interval for p₁ − p₂, the difference in the proportions of two cities’ residents who support a policy, is (−0.04, 0.10). Which conclusion is best?
  • (A) City 1 has a higher proportion of support.
  • (B) The proportions are equal.
  • (C) There is not convincing evidence of a difference in the proportions.
  • (D) There is a 95% chance the proportions are equal.
  • (E) 95% of residents in the two cities differ by between −4% and 10%.

0 is in the interval → a difference of 0 is plausible → not convincing evidence of a difference → (C).

(B) is too strong — we never prove equality.

Conditions for an experiment

Researchers randomly assign 60 patients to a new drug and 60 to a placebo. Which condition does NOT need to be checked before constructing a CI for p_{drug} − p_{placebo}?
  • (A) Random assignment
  • (B) The 10% condition
  • (C) At least 10 successes in each group
  • (D) At least 10 failures in each group
  • (E) Independent groups

The 10% condition is about sampling without replacement from a population. In a randomized experiment, the subjects were not sampled → (B).

📝 AP Practice 9 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 3.10

Changing the confidence level

A 95% CI for p₁ − p₂ is (0.02, 0.12). If the same data were used to build a 99% interval, the new interval would
  • (A) be narrower and centered at 0.07
  • (B) be wider and centered at 0.07
  • (C) be wider and centered at a different value
  • (D) be the same width
  • (E) definitely contain 0

The center is still p̂₁ − p̂₂ = 0.07; z* increases from 1.96 to 2.576 → wider → (B).

It might or might not contain 0 — (E) is not guaranteed.

Exit ticket 🎟️ 离堂小测

At a large high school, a random sample of 350 seniors and a separate random sample of 400 juniors were asked if they bring lunch from home. 161 seniors and 156 juniors said yes.

1Construct and interpret a 90% confidence interval for p_{S} − p_{J}.
Step 1. State: 90% CI for p_{S} − p_{J}. Plan: two-sample z interval; independent random samples ✓; 350 < 10% of all seniors and 400 < 10% of all juniors (assume the school is large enough) ✓; 161, 189, 156, 244 ≥ 10 ✓.
Step 2. Do: SE = √(0.46·0.54/350 + 0.39·0.61/400) ≈ 0.0361; 0.07 ± 1.645(0.0361) = 0.07 ± 0.059 → (0.011, 0.129). Conclude: We are 90% confident the interval from 0.011 to 0.129 captures the true difference (seniors − juniors) in the proportions who bring lunch from home.
2Is there convincing evidence that seniors are more likely to bring lunch? Explain.
Yes — the entire interval is above 0, so there is convincing evidence (at the 90% level) that seniors are more likely to bring lunch from home.
🔑 p̂_{S} = 0.46, p̂_{J} = 0.39.
