📝 Warm-up · 例题★★★★Warm-up

Breaking an addiction 戒除成瘾

In an experiment, 48 cocaine addicts were randomly assigned to take desipramine (24) or lithium (24). 10 of the desipramine group and 18 of the lithium group relapsed. 随机分配；复发人数 10 vs 18。

Q1Find the proportion who relapsed in each group and the difference p̂_{D} − p̂_{L}.
p̂_{D} = 10/24 ≈ 0.417, p̂_{L} = 18/24 = 0.75 → difference ≈ −0.333.
Q2If the two drugs were equally effective, could a difference this large happen just because of the random assignment?
Step 1. Imagine 28 “relapsers” and 20 “non-relapsers” no matter which drug they get. Re-randomize them into two groups of 24 many times and record p̂_{D} − p̂_{L} each time.
Step 2. A difference of −0.333 or smaller happened in only about 1% of re-randomizations → hard to explain by chance → convincing evidence desipramine works better.
🔑 Assume “no difference”, then ask how surprising the data are.
💡 That is a significance test for p₁ − p₂. Today we do it with the normal model.

Hypotheses for a Two-Sample z Test 两样本 z 检验的假设

Null hypothesis零假设
H₀: p₁ − p₂ = 0 (equivalently p₁ = p₂) — no difference between the two populations or treatments.
H₀: p₁ − p₂ = 0（即 p₁ = p₂）：两总体或两处理没有差异。
Alternative备择假设
Hₐ: p₁ − p₂ > 0, < 0, or ≠ 0. Define both parameters in context and say which is “1”.
Hₐ: p₁ − p₂ > 0、< 0 或 ≠ 0。在语境中定义两个参数，说明谁是 1。
p₁ = true proportion of (population / treatment 1) who (success); p₂ = true proportion of (population / treatment 2) who (success).

3.12 · Key ConceptsEK 3.12.C
Difficulty 难度 ★★★★

Pooled Proportion and Conditions 合并比例与条件

Pooled p̂c合并比例
If H₀ is true, both groups share one proportion. Estimate it by combining the samples: p̂c = (x₁ + x₂)/(n₁ + n₂) = total successes ÷ total sample size.
若 H₀ 为真，两组比例相同；合并两样本估计它：p̂c = (x₁ + x₂)/(n₁ + n₂)。
Conditions条件
Random: two independent random samples or random assignment. 10%: n₁ ≤ 0.10N₁, n₂ ≤ 0.10N₂ (sampling only). Large Counts: n₁p̂c, n₁(1 − p̂c), n₂p̂c, n₂(1 − p̂c) all ≥ 10.
随机：两个独立随机样本或随机分配。10%：仅抽样时检验。大计数：n₁p̂c、n₁(1−p̂c)、n₂p̂c、n₂(1−p̂c) 都 ≥ 10。
⚠️ Interval (Lesson 7): Large Counts with p̂₁ and p̂₂. Test (today): Large Counts with the pooled p̂c. 检验用合并比例。

📝 Example 2 · 例题★★★★EK 3.12

Hypotheses and pooled p̂c 假设与合并比例

For each study, state the hypotheses and find p̂c.

aCocaine experiment: does desipramine lead to a lower relapse rate than lithium? (10 of 24 vs. 18 of 24 relapsed)
a. H₀: p_{D} − p_{L} = 0, Hₐ: p_{D} − p_{L} < 0, where p_{D}, p_{L} = true relapse rates for addicts like these taking desipramine, lithium. p̂c = (10 + 18)/48 = 28/48 ≈ 0.583. Counts: 24(0.583) = 14, 24(0.417) = 10 → all ≥ 10 ✓.
bRandom samples of 200 teens in City A and 200 in City B: 62 and 48 have a part-time job. Is there a difference?
b. H₀: p_{A} − p_{B} = 0, Hₐ: p_{A} − p_{B} ≠ 0, where p_{A}, p_{B} = true proportions of all teens in City A, City B with a part-time job. p̂c = (62 + 48)/400 = 0.275. Counts: 55, 145, 55, 145 ≥ 10 ✓.
🔑 H₀ always says “no difference”.

The Two-Sample z Test Statistic 两样本 z 检验统计量

Test statistic检验统计量
z = (p̂₁ − p̂₂ − 0) / √(p̂c(1 − p̂c)(1/n₁ + 1/n₂)) — (statistic − null value)/standard error using the pooled p̂c. If H₀ is true, z is approximately standard normal.
z = (p̂₁ − p̂₂ − 0)/√(p̂c(1 − p̂c)(1/n₁ + 1/n₂))，用合并比例计算标准误；H₀ 为真时 z 近似标准正态。
p-valuep 值
From the standard normal: P(Z ≥ z), P(Z ≤ z), or 2P(Z ≥ |z|) depending on Hₐ. Calculator: STAT → TESTS → 6:2-PropZTest.
按 Hₐ 方向求右尾、左尾或双尾。计算器：2-PropZTest。
p̂c = (x₁ + x₂)/(n₁ + n₂)

Formula · 公式

The Two-Sample z Test Statistic 两样本 z 检验统计量

z=\frac{(\hat p_1-\hat p_2)-0}{\sqrt{\hat p_c(1-\hat p_c)\left(\frac1{n_1}+\frac1{n_2}\right)}}\qquad\hat p_c=\frac{x_1+x_2}{n_1+n_2}

Read the numerator and denominator separately, then identify each parameter or statistic in context. 先看分子和分母，再说明各符号的含义。

3.13 · Key ConceptsEK 3.13.B–C
Difficulty 难度 ★★★★

Conclusions: Surveys vs. Experiments 结论：调查与实验

Decision决策
p-value ≤ α → reject H₀: convincing evidence of a difference (in the direction of Hₐ). p-value > α → fail to reject H₀: not convincing evidence. Never “accept” H₀.
p ≤ α → 拒绝 H₀；p > α → 不拒绝 H₀。不说“接受”。
Scope推断范围
Random samples → generalize to the populations. Random assignment (experiment) → a significant result supports cause and effect for subjects like these.
随机抽样 → 推广到总体；随机分配（实验）→ 显著结果可支持因果结论（对类似受试者）。

📝 Example 3 · 例题★★★★EK 3.13 · Skill 4.G

Does preschool help? 学前教育有帮助吗？

In the Perry Preschool experiment, 123 low-income children were randomly assigned to attend preschool (62) or not (61). As adults, 38 of the preschool group and 49 of the control group needed social services.

Do the data give convincing evidence at α = 0.05 that preschool reduces the later need for social services? Carry out a test.
Step 1. State: H₀: p_{P} − p_{C} = 0, Hₐ: p_{P} − p_{C} < 0, α = 0.05; p_{P}, p_{C} = true proportions of children like these who would need social services with / without preschool.
Step 2. Plan: two-sample z test for p₁ − p₂. Random assignment ✓. ✓.
Step 3. Do: p̂_{P} = 0.613, p̂_{C} = 0.803; z = (0.613 − 0.803)/√(0.707·0.293(1/62 + 1/61)) ≈ −0.190/0.0821 ≈ −2.32; p-value = P(Z ≤ −2.32) ≈ 0.010: assuming preschool makes no difference, there is about a 0.010 probability of getting a difference of −0.19 or less by chance in the random assignment.
Step 4. Conclude: 0.010 < 0.05 → reject H₀. Convincing evidence that preschool causes a lower rate of needing social services for children like these (random assignment).
🔑 State → Plan → Do → Conclude.

📝 Example 4 · 例题★★★★EK 3.13 · Skill 4.G

Part-time jobs (two-sided) 兼职工作（双侧）

Random samples of 200 teens in City A and 200 teens in City B: 62 in A and 48 in B have a part-time job (p̂c = 0.275). Both cities have more than 2000 teens.

aCalculate the test statistic and p-value for H₀: p_{A} − p_{B} = 0 vs. Hₐ: p_{A} − p_{B} ≠ 0.
Step 1. p̂_{A} = 0.31, p̂_{B} = 0.24. z = (0.31 − 0.24)/√(0.275·0.725(1/200 + 1/200)) = 0.07/0.0447 ≈ 1.57.
Step 2. p-value = 2P(Z ≥ 1.57) ≈ 0.117.
bWhat do you conclude at α = 0.05? Which type of error could you have made?
Step 1. 0.117 > 0.05 → fail to reject H₀. Not convincing evidence that the proportions of teens with a part-time job differ in the two cities.
Step 2. We failed to reject H₀, so we could have made a Type II error: the proportions really differ, but we did not find convincing evidence. (This is an observational study — no cause-and-effect claims anyway.)
🔑 ≠ → two tails.

📝 Example 5 · 例题★★★★EK 3.13 · 3.11

Hearing loss: test vs. interval 听力损失：检验与区间

Lesson 7: 345 of 1771 teens (2005–06) vs. 436 of 2928 teens (1988–94) had some hearing loss; the 95% CI for p_{2005} − p_{1988} was (0.023, 0.069).

aTest H₀: p_{2005} − p_{1988} = 0 vs. Hₐ: p_{2005} − p_{1988} > 0 (p̂c ≈ 0.166).
z = (0.195 − 0.149)/√(0.166·0.834(1/1771 + 1/2928)) ≈ 0.046/0.0112 ≈ 4.10; p-value ≈ 0.00002 → reject H₀: convincing evidence that hearing loss became more common.
bHow do the test and the interval agree? What extra information does the interval give?
The interval does not contain 0 (all positive) — same conclusion. The interval also estimates how much: an increase of about 2.3 to 6.9 percentage points.
🔑 Test: yes/no. Interval: how big.

Correct hypotheses

A researcher wants to know whether the proportion of left-handed people differs between men and women. Which hypotheses are correct?
  • (A) H₀: p̂_{M} = p̂_{W}; Hₐ: p̂_{M} ≠ p̂_{W}
  • (B) H₀: p_{M} − p_{W} = 0; Hₐ: p_{M} − p_{W} > 0
  • (C) H₀: p_{M} − p_{W} = 0; Hₐ: p_{M} − p_{W} ≠ 0
  • (D) H₀: p_{M} − p_{W} ≠ 0; Hₐ: p_{M} − p_{W} = 0
  • (E) H₀: p_{M} − p_{W} > 0; Hₐ: p_{M} − p_{W} = 0

Parameters (p, not p̂), equality in H₀, ≠ for “differs” → (C).

Cause and effect

A random sample of 500 smokers and a random sample of 500 nonsmokers show a significantly higher rate of heart disease among smokers (p-value = 0.002). Which conclusion is appropriate?
  • (A) Smoking causes heart disease.
  • (B) There is convincing evidence that the proportion with heart disease is higher for all smokers than for all nonsmokers, but this study cannot show causation.
  • (C) There is a 0.2% chance that smoking has no effect.
  • (D) The result can only be applied to the 1000 people in the study.
  • (E) Fail to reject H₀ because the samples are observational.

Random samples → generalize to the populations. No random assignment → no cause-and-effect conclusion → (B).

(C) misinterprets the p-value.

Type I error

A company tests H₀: p_{new} − p_{old} = 0 vs. Hₐ: p_{new} − p_{old} > 0, where p is the proportion of customers who click on an ad. A Type I error would be:
  • (A) Concluding the new ad gets more clicks when it really does
  • (B) Concluding the new ad gets more clicks when it really does not
  • (C) Not concluding the new ad gets more clicks when it really does
  • (D) Not concluding the new ad gets more clicks when it really does not
  • (E) Using α = 0.05 instead of 0.01

Reject H₀ (conclude new is better) when H₀ is true (no difference) → (B). Consequence: switching to a new ad that is not actually better.

(C) is a Type II error.

Exit ticket 🎟️ 离堂小测

A school wants more parents to return permission slips. It randomly assigns 300 families: 150 get a text reminder, 150 do not. 54 reminded families and 39 others return the slip on time.

1Do the data give convincing evidence at α = 0.05 that text reminders increase the return rate? (four steps)
Step 1. State: H₀: p_{R} − p_{N} = 0, Hₐ: p_{R} − p_{N} > 0, α = 0.05; p = true return rate for families like these with / without a reminder. Plan: two-sample z test; random assignment ✓; 150(0.31) = 46.5, 150(0.69) = 103.5 ≥ 10 ✓.
Step 2. Do: z = (0.36 − 0.26)/√(0.31·0.69(1/150 + 1/150)) = 0.10/0.0534 ≈ 1.87; p-value = P(Z ≥ 1.87) ≈ 0.031.
Step 3. Conclude: 0.031 < 0.05 → reject H₀. Convincing evidence that text reminders increase the return rate.
2Can the school conclude that the reminders caused the increase? Explain.
Yes — families were randomly assigned to the treatments, so the significant difference can be attributed to the reminders (for families like these).
🔑 p̂_{R} = 0.36, p̂_{N} = 0.26, p̂c = 93/300 = 0.31.
