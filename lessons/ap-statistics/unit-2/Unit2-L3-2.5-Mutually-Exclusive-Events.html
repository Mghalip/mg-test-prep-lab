Joint Probability 联合概率

Definition定义
The probability that events A and B both occur is called the joint probability. It is the probability of the intersection of A and B, written P(A ∩ B) ("A intersect B", "A and B").
事件 A 和 B 同时发生的概率叫作联合概率，即 A 与 B 的交集的概率，记作 P(A ∩ B)，读作 “A 交 B” 或 “A 且 B”。
🔗 Lesson 1 link: a joint relative frequency (cell ÷ grand total) becomes a joint probability when we choose an individual at random.

📝 Example 2 · 例题★★★★EK 2.5.A.1

Find the joint probability 求联合概率

aDraw one card. Find P(heart ∩ king).
Only the K♥ is both → 1/52.
bRoll two dice. Find P(doubles ∩ sum is 8).
Doubles (navy) and sum 8 (pink): only (4,4) is both (gold) → 1/36.
cRoll two dice. Find P(sum is 7 ∩ doubles).
Doubles always give an even sum, so no cell is both → 0/36 = 0.
🔑 Joint probability = outcomes in BOTH events ÷ total.
💡 P(A ∩ B) = 0 means A and B never happen together — the topic of today!

Mutually Exclusive (Disjoint) Events 互斥事件

Definition定义
Two events are mutually exclusive, or disjoint, if they cannot occur at the same time — they have no outcomes in common. If A and B are mutually exclusive, then P(A ∩ B) = 0.
如果两个事件不可能同时发生（没有共同的结果），就称它们互斥（不相交）。互斥 ⇔ 联合概率 P(A ∩ B) = 0。
No overlap → A ∩ B is empty
两个圆不重叠

📝 Example 4 · 例题★★★★EK 2.5.A.2

Mutually exclusive or not? 互斥吗？

Roll one fair die.

aA = "even", B = "odd". Mutually exclusive?
A = {2, 4, 6}, B = {1, 3, 5} → no common outcome → mutually exclusive, P(A ∩ B) = 0.
bA = "even", C = "greater than 3". Mutually exclusive?
A = {2, 4, 6}, C = {4, 5, 6} → 4 and 6 are in both → not mutually exclusive.
cFind P(A ∩ C).
P(A ∩ C) = 2/6 ≈ 0.333 ≠ 0.
🔑 One die: even, odd, greater than 3.

How to justify "mutually exclusive" 如何论证是否互斥

Sentence frame 句型

Events A and B are / are not mutually exclusive because P(A ∩ B) = ___, which is / is not equal to 0. [In context: it is / is not possible for an individual to be both A and B.]

Checklist ✔

  • Compute the joint probability P(A ∩ B)
  • Compare it with 0
  • Conclusion in context
“They can’t happen together” alone is not enough — show the number. 要写出联合概率。

Use the pierced-ears table (choose a student at random).

aAre “male” and “pierced ears” mutually exclusive? Justify.
No. P(male ∩ pierced ears) = 19/178 ≈ 0.107, which is not 0. In this class, 19 students are both male and have pierced ears.
bAre “male” and “female” mutually exclusive? Justify.
Yes. No student is in both the Male row and the Female row, so P(male ∩ female) = 0/178 = 0.

Addition Rule for Mutually Exclusive Events 互斥事件的加法法则

Rule法则
If A and B are mutually exclusive, the probability that one or the other occurs is the sum of their probabilities: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B).
若 A 和 B 互斥，则 “A 或 B 发生” 的概率等于两者概率之和。
Unit 2 · Lesson 3
📝 Example 6 · 例题★★★★TPS 5.2

Add disjoint probabilities 互斥相加

aOne die: P(1 or 6).
1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6 ≈ 0.333
bBlood type (Lesson 2): P(O or A) with P(O) = 0.45, P(A) = 0.40.
0.45 + 0.40 = 0.85
cTwo dice: P(sum is 7 or sum is 11).
6/36 + 2/36 = 8/36 ≈ 0.222
dWhy is it OK to just add in each case?
Each pair is mutually exclusive: one roll can’t be both 1 and 6; a person has one blood type; one sum can’t be 7 and 11.
🔑 Disjoint events: just add.

General Addition Rule 一般加法法则

Rule法则
For any two events A and B, the probability that A or B (or both) occurs — the probability of the union — is P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B).
对任意两个事件，“A 或 B（或两者都）发生” 即并集的概率 = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)。减去交集是因为它被重复计算了一次。
P(A) + P(B) counts the overlap twice → subtract it once.
If A and B are mutually exclusive, P(A ∩ B) = 0 and this becomes the simple addition rule. 互斥时就变回简单加法法则。

Same class of 178 students. Find P(male or pierced ears) in three different ways:

③the complement rule

Rules · AP Exam Tips

Common mistakes 常见错误

✗ Mistake 1

Double counting

Using P(A) + P(B) for "A or B" when A and B overlap.

不互斥时直接相加，重复计算了交集。

✗ Mistake 2

Mixing up ∪ and ∩

∪ = union = "or" (either one, or both). ∩ = intersection = "and" (both).

把“并”和“交”搞混。

✗ Mistake 3

Justifying without numbers

"They can't happen together" — also show P(A ∩ B) = 0 (or ≠ 0).

论证互斥时要写出联合概率。

✗ Mistake 4

"Or" means "only one"

In statistics, "A or B" includes outcomes in both.

统计里的“或”包括两者都发生。

Seniors and AP Statistics 高年级与 AP 统计（mini-FRQ）

A random sample of 200 students. Choose one at random. Let S = is a senior, T = takes AP Statistics.

aFind P(S ∩ T).
P(S ∩ T) = 42/200 = 0.21
bAre S and T mutually exclusive? Justify.
No. P(S ∩ T) = 0.21 ≠ 0 — 42 students are seniors who take AP Statistics.
cFind P(S ∪ T).
P(S ∪ T) = 80/200 + 60/200 − 42/200 = 98/200 = 0.49
dFind P(neither a senior nor in AP Stats).
P(neither) = 1 − 0.49 = 0.51 (check: 102/200 ✓)

📝 AP Practice 9 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 2.5.A.2 · 2.7.A.3

Is it mutually exclusive?

Events A and B have P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.3, and P(A ∪ B) = 0.6. Which statement is true?
  • (A) A and B are mutually exclusive because P(A ∪ B) < 1.
  • (B) A and B are mutually exclusive because P(A) + P(B) = 0.7.
  • (C) A and B are not mutually exclusive because P(A ∩ B) = 0.1.
  • (D) A and B are not mutually exclusive because P(A ∩ B) = 0.12.
  • (E) There is not enough information to decide.

General addition rule: 0.6 = 0.4 + 0.3 − P(A ∩ B).

P(A ∩ B) = 0.1 ≠ 0 → not mutually exclusive → (C).

(D) multiplies 0.4 × 0.3 — that needs independence (next lesson!).

(D) {1, 2} and {5, 6} share nothing → (D).

📝 AP Practice 11 · AP 选择题★★★★CED 2.7.A.3 · 2.4.A.4

Languages

At a school of 500 students, 120 take French, 80 take Spanish, and 20 take both. A student is chosen at random. What is the probability the student takes neither language?
  • (A) 0.36
  • (B) 0.60
  • (C) 0.64
  • (D) 0.68
  • (E) 0.96

Fill the Venn diagram: French only 100, both 20, Spanish only 60.

P(F ∪ S) = (120 + 80 − 20)/500 = 0.36.

P(neither) = 1 − 0.36 = 0.64 → (C). (B) forgets to subtract the overlap; (A) is the union itself.

Exit ticket 🎟️ 离堂小测

For a randomly chosen student: P(owns a cat) = 0.30, P(owns a dog) = 0.55, and P(owns a cat or a dog) = 0.70.

1Find P(owns a cat and a dog).
0.70 = 0.30 + 0.55 − P(C ∩ D) → P(C ∩ D) = 0.15
2Are “owns a cat” and “owns a dog” mutually exclusive? Justify.
No — P(C ∩ D) = 0.15 ≠ 0, so some students own both.
3Find P(owns neither).
P(neither) = 1 − 0.70 = 0.30
