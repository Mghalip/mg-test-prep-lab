📝 Warm-up · 例题★★★★Warm-up

Girls vs. boys 女孩与男孩的身高

Heights of 10-year-old girls: normal, μ_{G} = 56.4 in, σ_{G} = 2.7 in. Boys: normal, μ_{B} = 55.7 in, σ_{B} = 3.8 in. Take independent SRSs of 12 girls and 8 boys.

Q1On average, which group’s sample mean will be larger? By how much?
On average x̄_{G} − x̄_{B} = μ_{G} − μ_{B} = 56.4 − 55.7 = 0.7 inch.
Q2Could the boys’ sample mean be larger than the girls’? Why?
Yes — both sample means vary, so the difference varies a lot (SD ≈ 1.55 in). x̄_{B} > x̄_{G} happens about 33% of the time.
🔑 x̄_{G} − x̄_{B} varies around μ_{G} − μ_{B}.

4.6.A · Key ConceptsEK 4.6.A.1
Difficulty 难度 ★★★★

Center and Spread of x̄₁ − x̄₂ x̄₁ − x̄₂ 的中心与离散程度

Mean均值
μ_{x̄₁−x̄₂} = μ₁ − μ₂ — the difference of sample means is an unbiased estimator of the difference of population means.
μ_{x̄₁−x̄₂} = μ₁ − μ₂（无偏估计）。
Standard deviation标准差
σ_{x̄₁−x̄₂} = √(σ₁²/n₁ + σ₂²/n₂) for independent samples. Variances add (even though we subtract the means).
独立样本时 σ = √(σ₁²/n₁ + σ₂²/n₂)。方差相加（虽然均值相减）。
\sigma_{\bar x_1-\bar x_2}=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}
p̂₁ − p̂₂: √(p₁(1 − p₁)/n₁ + p₂(1 − p₂)/n₂) — same idea. 与比例差同理。

Difficulty 难度 ★★★★

Conditions and Shape 条件与形状

Conditions条件
Random: two independent random samples. 10%: n₁ ≤ 10% N₁ and n₂ ≤ 10% N₂. For an experiment, only random assignment of treatments is needed.
随机：两个独立随机样本；10%：每个样本各自检验。实验只需随机分配处理。
Shape形状
Normal if both population distributions are normal (any n). Approximately normal if not normal but n₁ ≥ 30 and n₂ ≥ 30.
两个总体都正态 → 正态（任何 n）；否则需 n₁ ≥ 30 且 n₂ ≥ 30 → 近似正态。

📝 Example 2 · 例题★★★★EK 4.6 · Skill 3.D

Heights: probabilities 身高：求概率

Girls: N(56.4, 2.7); boys: N(55.7, 3.8). Independent SRSs of 12 girls and 8 boys.

aDescribe the shape, center, and spread of the sampling distribution of x̄_{G} − x̄_{B}.
Shape: normal (both populations normal). Center: 56.4 − 55.7 = 0.7 in. Spread: √(2.7²/12 + 3.8²/8) ≈ 1.553 in (12 and 8 are less than 10% of all 10-year-olds ✓).
bFind the probability that the girls’ sample mean is at least 2 inches larger than the boys’.
z = (2 − 0.7)/1.553 ≈ 0.84 → P ≈ 0.201.
cFind P(x̄_{G} − x̄_{B} < 0) and explain what it means.
z = (0 − 0.7)/1.553 ≈ −0.45 → P ≈ 0.326: in about 33% of such pairs of samples, the boys’ sample mean height would be larger than the girls’.
🔑 Both populations normal → normal for any n.

📝 Example 3 · 例题★★★★EK 4.6.A · 4.6.C

Coffee cups 咖啡杯

A café’s large cups hold an amount that is normal with mean 24 oz and SD 0.4 oz; medium cups: normal, mean 14 oz, SD 0.3 oz. A manager randomly selects 10 large and 10 medium cups.

aFind and interpret the standard deviation of x̄_{L} − x̄_{M}.
σ = √(0.4²/10 + 0.3²/10) ≈ 0.158 oz. The difference (large − medium) in sample mean amounts typically varies by about 0.16 oz from the true difference of 10 oz.
bFind the probability that the difference in sample means is less than 9.8 oz.
Normal (both populations normal), mean 10. z = (9.8 − 10)/0.158 ≈ −1.26 → P ≈ 0.103.
🔑 Variances add.

📝 Example 4 · 例题★★★★EK 4.6.B.3–4

Normal model or not? 可以用正态模型吗？

Can we use a normal model for x̄₁ − x̄₂? Explain.

aCommute times (right-skewed) for random samples of 40 workers in City A and 50 in City B.
a. Yes — n₁ = 40 ≥ 30 and n₂ = 50 ≥ 30, so x̄₁ − x̄₂ is approximately normal.
bHousehold incomes (strongly right-skewed) for random samples of 15 households in each of two towns.
b. No — both populations are skewed and both samples are small (15 < 30).
cTest scores (normal) for random samples of 8 students at each of two schools.
c. Yes — both populations are normal, so the sampling distribution is normal for any sample sizes.
🔑 Both normal → yes; otherwise both n ≥ 30.

📝 AP Practice 5 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 4.6.A.1

SD of the difference

Population 1 has σ₁ = 6 and population 2 has σ₂ = 8. For independent SRSs with n₁ = 9 and n₂ = 16, the SD of x̄₁ − x̄₂ is
  • (A) 2 − 2 = 0
  • (B) 2 + 2 = 4
  • (C) √(4 + 4) ≈ 2.83
  • (D) √(36 + 64) = 10
  • (E) √(6/9 + 8/16) ≈ 1.08

√(36/9 + 64/16) = √(4 + 4) ≈ 2.83 → (C). (A) and (B) combine SDs instead of variances.

Shape condition

Two populations are strongly skewed. For which sample sizes is x̄₁ − x̄₂ approximately normal?
  • (A) n₁ = 10, n₂ = 10
  • (B) n₁ = 50, n₂ = 20
  • (C) n₁ = 35, n₂ = 40
  • (D) n₁ = 25, n₂ = 60
  • (E) none — skewed populations never give a normal model

Both samples must be at least 30 → (C).

📝 Exit ticket · 例题★★★★4.6

Exit ticket 🎟️ 离堂小测

Commute times in City A: mean 28 min, SD 12 min; City B: mean 25 min, SD 15 min (both right-skewed). Random samples of 40 workers in A and 50 in B.

1Describe the sampling distribution of x̄_{A} − x̄_{B}.
Approximately normal (40 ≥ 30, 50 ≥ 30); mean 28 − 25 = 3 min; SD √(12²/40 + 15²/50) ≈ 2.85 min.
2Find the probability that City B’s sample mean is larger than City A’s.
P(x̄_{A} − x̄_{B} < 0): z = (0 − 3)/2.85 ≈ −1.05 → P ≈ 0.146.
🔑 Both n ≥ 30 → approximately normal.
