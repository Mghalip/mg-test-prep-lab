📝 Warm-up · 例题★★★★Warm-up

Does caffeine raise your pulse? 咖啡因会让心跳变快吗？

To see whether caffeine increases pulse rate, a teacher asks 20 student volunteers to measure their pulse, drink a cola with caffeine, wait 10 minutes, and measure their pulse again. Every student’s pulse rate went up. 20 名学生喝含咖啡因可乐后心率都上升了。

Q1Can the teacher conclude that caffeine caused the increase?
No. There is no comparison group, so other variables are confounded with caffeine.
Q2What other explanations are there? How could you fix the design?
Step 1. Maybe the sugar in the cola, the excitement of being in an experiment, or walking to class raised pulses — we can’t separate these from caffeine.
Step 2. Fix: compare two groups — randomly assign half to cola with caffeine and half to caffeine-free cola (same taste, same sugar), then compare the changes.
🔑 Students → cola with caffeine → measure pulse.
💡 No comparison → no way to tell what caused the change.

A well-designed experiment includes… 设计良好的实验包括

Control Group & the Placebo Effect 对照组与安慰剂效应

Control group对照组
A control group is a collection of experimental units created for comparison. It may get a different treatment (an active treatment, no treatment, or an inactive substance — a placebo) to determine whether the treatment of interest has an effect.
对照组：为了比较而设的一组实验单位。可以接受另一种处理、不处理，或接受无效成分的安慰剂。
Placebo effect安慰剂效应
The placebo effect is the difference between the average response to a placebo and the average response to no treatment — people often respond to any treatment, even a fake one.
安慰剂效应：接受安慰剂的平均反应与不接受处理的平均反应之差——人们常对任何“治疗”有反应，即使是假的。

Blinding 盲法

Single-blind单盲
In a single-blind (single-masked) experiment, the participants don’t know which treatment they are receiving, but the research team members who interact with them do — or vice versa.
单盲：受试者不知道自己接受哪种处理，但与其接触的研究人员知道（或反过来）。
Double-blind双盲
In a double-blind (double-masked) experiment, neither the participants nor the team members who interact with them (and measure the response) know which treatment each participant is receiving.
双盲：受试者和与其接触（测量响应）的研究人员都不知道谁接受了哪种处理。

📝 Example 2 · 例题★★★★EK 1.13.A.2–5

Testing a new pain reliever 测试新止痛药

A company wants to know whether its new pain reliever reduces headache pain more than taking nothing. 120 adults with frequent headaches volunteer.

aWhy should the control group get a placebo rather than nothing?
People who believe they got medicine may report less pain anyway (placebo effect). With a placebo, both groups expect relief, so a difference can be credited to the drug.
bExplain how to make the experiment double-blind, and why it matters here.
Step 1. Use identical-looking pills with code numbers; a third party keeps the key. Subjects don’t know their treatment, and the nurses who record pain ratings don’t either.
Step 2. Double-blinding prevents subjects’ expectations and researchers’ expectations (in how they treat subjects or record pain) from affecting the response.
🔑 Pain is self-reported — expectations matter!

Extraneous Variables & Random Assignment 外部变量与随机分配

Extraneous variable外部变量
An extraneous variable (source of variation) is known or believed to affect the response but is not an explanatory variable being studied.
外部变量：已知或认为会影响响应，但不是本研究的解释变量。
Purpose of random assignment随机分配的目的
Random assignment creates treatment groups that are as similar as possible with respect to extraneous variables. If it is successful, the distribution of each extraneous variable is about the same in all groups.
随机分配让各处理组在外部变量上尽可能相似；若成功，每个外部变量在各组中的分布大致相同。
Confounding in experiments实验中的混杂
A confounding variable is related to the explanatory variable so that we can’t tell which one is influencing the response. In a well-designed experiment, the potential for confounding is reduced.
混杂变量与解释变量相关，以致无法分辨是谁影响了响应。设计良好的实验能减少混杂的可能。

Replication & Direct Control 重复与直接控制

Replication重复
Replication within an experiment means more than one experimental unit is assigned to each treatment — enough to tell a real treatment effect apart from chance differences.
重复：每种处理分配给多个实验单位，足以把真实效应与偶然差异区分开。
Direct control直接控制
Direct control means keeping the settings of certain potential extraneous variables the same from unit to unit (same cola amount, same waiting time, same room…). It reduces variability in the response and helps avoid confounding.
直接控制：让某些外部变量对每个实验单位都保持相同（同样的饮料量、等待时间、房间……），减少响应的变异，避免混杂。

📝 Example 3 · 例题★★★★EK 1.13.A.1.ii · TPS 4.2

Assigning the cola 分配可乐

20 students will compare caffeinated cola and caffeine-free cola on pulse rate. Describe how to randomly assign 10 students to each treatment using

a20 identical slips of paper
Slips: write “A” on 10 slips and “B” on 10 slips; shuffle in a hat; each student draws one. A → caffeinated cola, B → caffeine-free.
btechnology (a random number generator)
Step 1. Technology: label students 1–20; generate random integers from 1 to 20, ignoring repeats, until 10 different labels are chosen → caffeinated; the other 10 → caffeine-free.
Step 2. Then: same amount of cola, same 10-minute wait, same way of measuring pulse (direct control).
🔑 Random assignment → equal-sized groups.

📝 Example 4 · 例题★★★★EK 1.13.A.1 · TPS 4.3

Cell phones and driving 开车打电话

Researchers at the University of Utah used 48 undergraduate students. They randomly assigned half to drive in a simulator while talking on a cell phone and the other half to drive in the same simulator while talking to a passenger. The response: whether the driver stopped at a rest area specified before the simulation started.

Identify how each of the four principles is (or could be) used.
Step 1. Comparison: cell phone vs. passenger — two treatment groups.
Step 2. Random assignment: chance decided who got which distraction → groups similar in driving skill, attention, etc.
Step 3. Replication: 24 students in each group.
Step 4. Direct control: same simulator, same route and rest-area instructions for everyone.

Completely Randomized Design 完全随机设计

Completely randomized完全随机
In a completely randomized design, treatments are assigned to experimental units completely at random. Group sizes are often equal but do not have to be.
完全随机设计：把处理完全随机地分配给实验单位；各组人数常相等，但不必相等。

1.13.B · Key ConceptsEK 1.13.B.2–3
Difficulty 难度 ★★★★

Randomized Block Design 随机区组设计

Block区组
A blocking variable is a source of extraneous variation. Units are first grouped by similar values of the blocking variable into blocks (homogeneous). Then treatments are randomly assigned within each block, so all treatments occur in every block.
区组变量是外部变异的来源。先按区组变量把相似的实验单位分成区组，再在每个区组内随机分配处理。
Purpose目的
Blocking separates the variation caused by the blocking variable from the rest → more precise comparisons of the treatments.
区组把由区组变量造成的变异分离出来 → 处理之间的比较更精确。

Matched Pairs Design 配对设计

Matched pairs配对设计
A matched pairs design is a randomized block design with two treatments (blocks of size 2). Either ① pair similar units and randomly assign one treatment to each member, or ② each unit gets both treatments in a random order.
配对设计是只有两种处理的随机区组设计。① 把相似的两个单位配成一对，随机决定谁接受哪种处理；或 ② 每个单位两种处理都接受，顺序随机。

📝 Example 5 · 例题★★★★EK 1.13.B.2–3

Diets for men and women 男女分区组的减肥实验

Researchers want to compare two diet programs (A and B) for weight loss. They have 30 men and 20 women. They expect men and women to respond differently to diets.

aDescribe a randomized block design for this experiment.
Step 1. Form two blocks: men (30) and women (20).
Step 2. Within the men block, randomly assign 15 to diet A and 15 to diet B; within the women block, randomly assign 10 to each. After the program, compare weight loss for A vs. B within each block, then combine.
bWhy is blocking by sex better than a completely randomized design here?
Sex affects weight loss, so blocking removes that variation from the comparison → more precise estimate of the diet effect.
🔑 Block = sex (an extraneous variable).

📝 Example 6 · 例题★★★★EK 1.13.B.4

Laptop or tablet? 笔记本还是平板？

Do students type faster on a laptop keyboard or on a tablet screen? 30 students are available.

aDescribe a matched pairs design.
Each student types the same passage on both devices. Flip a coin for each student: heads → laptop first, tails → tablet first. Record words per minute on each; compute the difference (laptop − tablet) for each student.
bWhy is randomizing the order important?
Students may get faster with practice (or tired); randomizing the order keeps order effects from being confounded with the device.
cWhy is this design better than splitting the students into two groups?
Typing skill varies a lot between students. Comparing each student with themselves removes that variation → more precise comparison.
🔑 Each student is their own pair.

📝 Example 7 · 例题★★★★EK 1.13.C.1 · Skill 2.B

Which design is better? 哪种设计更好？

A company wants to compare two sunscreens (X and Y) for preventing sunburn. 40 volunteers are available; their skin types range from very fair to dark.

Design 1Randomly assign 20 volunteers to sunscreen X and 20 to sunscreen Y. Each person applies it to their back and spends an hour in the sun.
Design 2Each volunteer applies X to one arm and Y to the other, with a coin flip deciding which arm gets X. Each person spends an hour in the sun.
Which design is better? Justify your answer.
Step 1. Skin type (and sun exposure) strongly affects sunburn and varies a lot between volunteers.
Step 2. Design 2 (matched pairs) compares X and Y on the same person → skin type and time in the sun are the same for both treatments.
Step 3. Randomizing which arm gets X balances arm differences (e.g., the arm facing the sun more).
Step 4. So Design 2 gives a more precise comparison than Design 1, where differences in skin type between the groups add variability.
🔑 Think: what extraneous variable matters most?
💡 Choose the design that removes the biggest source of extraneous variation.

Cause and Effect: Scope of Inference 因果结论：推断范围

Cause and effect因果关系
Random assignment of treatments allows cause-and-effect conclusions between the explanatory and response variables because the potential for confounding is reduced.
对实验单位随机分配处理，减少了混杂的可能，所以可以得出因果结论。
Volunteers自愿者
It may be unethical or difficult to randomly select units for an experiment. Then the units are volunteers, and the results apply to a population of units similar to those in the study.
在实验中随机选取个体常常不道德或很难做到，所以常用自愿者，结论只适用于与研究对象相似的个体。

📝 Example 8 · 例题★★★★EK 1.13.D · Skill 2.B

What can we conclude? 能得出什么结论？

A researcher recruits 80 volunteers from a local gym. She randomly assigns 40 to a new stretching routine and 40 to their usual routine. After 6 weeks, flexibility improved much more in the stretching group, and the difference was too large to be explained by chance.

aCan she conclude that the routine caused the improvement? Explain.
Yes — treatments were randomly assigned, so the groups should be similar in other variables; the difference was too big for chance → the routine caused the improvement.
bTo whom can the results be generalized?
Only to people similar to these gym volunteers — they were not randomly selected from a larger population.
🔑 Random assignment ✓ · random selection ✗.

Purpose of random assignment

In an experiment, the main purpose of randomly assigning subjects to treatments is to
  • (A) make sure the sample represents the population
  • (B) create groups that are roughly equivalent with respect to extraneous variables
  • (C) make the experiment double-blind
  • (D) increase the sample size
  • (E) eliminate the placebo effect

Random assignment balances extraneous variables among the treatment groups → roughly equivalent groups → (B).

(A) describes random selection; blinding and placebos are separate ideas.

Which kind of blinding?

In a study of a new allergy medicine, patients receive either the medicine or a placebo that looks identical. The patients do not know which they received, but the doctor who examines them does. This experiment is
  • (A) double-blind
  • (B) single-blind
  • (C) not blind
  • (D) a matched pairs design
  • (E) an observational study

Patients don’t know, but the doctor who interacts with them and measures the response does → single-blind → (B).

To be double-blind, the examining doctor must not know either.

A farmer tests 3 fertilizers on 30 plots on a hillside. She groups the plots into 10 blocks of 3 by elevation and randomly assigns the 3 fertilizers within each block. The main purpose of blocking is to
  • (A) remove the need for random assignment

Statistically significant? 结果统计显著吗？（Bonus）

In the cell-phone driving experiment: 21 of 24 passenger-talkers stopped at the rest area vs. 12 of 24 cell-phone talkers.

aFind the difference in proportions (passenger − cell phone).
21/24 − 12/24 = 0.875 − 0.500 = 0.375.
bIf the type of distraction made no difference, 33 “stoppers” would be split into two groups of 24 at random. Use the simulator on the next slide: how often is the difference ≥ 0.375?
In the TPS simulation of 100 random re-assignments, a difference of 0.375 or more never occurred (try the simulator!).
cIs the result statistically significant?
So the difference is statistically significant: very unlikely to happen just from the random assignment → talking on a cell phone causes fewer drivers to stop.
🔑 Statistically significant = too unusual to be explained by chance alone.

Basic data ethics 基本数据伦理

1

Review board

All planned studies must be reviewed in advance by an institutional review board that protects the safety and well-being of subjects.

伦理审查委员会事先审查。

2

Informed consent

All subjects must give their informed consent before data are collected.

知情同意。

3

Confidentiality

Individual data must be kept confidential; only statistical summaries for groups may be made public.

个人数据保密，只公布汇总结果。

This is why many experiments use volunteers (CED 1.13.D.2): we can’t force randomly selected people into a treatment. 这也是实验常用自愿者的原因。

Exit ticket 🎟️ 离堂小测

A teacher wants to know whether listening to classical music while studying improves memory test scores. 40 student volunteers: 20 from her AP class and 20 from a regular class.

1Describe a randomized block design. What is the blocking variable and why use it?
Blocks: AP class (20) and regular class (20). Within each block, randomly assign 10 to study with classical music and 10 in silence; compare mean scores within each block. AP students probably score higher anyway, so blocking removes that variation.
2Name one variable to control directly.
Control: same word list, same study time, same room, same test.
3If music students score much higher, what can she conclude, and about whom?
If the difference is too large to be due to chance: music caused higher scores (random assignment) — but only for students similar to these volunteers (no random selection).
🔑 Block = class (AP vs. regular).
