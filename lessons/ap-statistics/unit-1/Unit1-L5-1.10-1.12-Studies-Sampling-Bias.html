📝 Warm-up · 例题★★★★Warm-up

The biggest poll ever 史上最大的民调

In 1936 the magazine Literary Digest mailed questionnaires to 10 million people (taken from telephone books and car registration lists) about the U.S. presidential election. About 2.4 million replied. 《文学文摘》寄出 1000 万份问卷，收回 240 万份。

The poll predicted Alf Landon would win with 57% of the vote. Franklin Roosevelt actually won with 62%.

QWith 2.4 million responses, how could the poll be so wrong?
Step 1. In 1936 (the Great Depression), people with telephones and cars were wealthier and more likely to vote Republican (Landon) → part of the population was left out: undercoverage.
Step 2. Only 24% replied; those who replied may have had stronger anti-Roosevelt feelings: nonresponse.
Step 3. A huge sample does not fix a biased method. How the sample is chosen matters more than how big it is.
🔑 2.4 million responses — and still wrong.
💡 Large sample + bad method = precisely wrong. 样本大不能消除偏差。

Components of an Investigative Question 调查问题的三个组成部分

① Data collection① 数据收集
The first component should guide the data collection and be phrased in terms of the variable(s) of interest.
第一部分指导数据收集，用研究的变量来表述。
② Data analysis② 数据分析
The second component should guide the analysis. For a hypothesis test: make clear the parameter and the direction (≠, >, <, association). For a confidence interval: make clear the parameter and the goal of estimating it.
第二部分指导分析方法。假设检验：说明参数与备择方向（≠、>、<、关联）；置信区间：说明参数及估计目标。
③ Conclusion③ 结论类型
The third component indicates the type of conclusion: the population the conclusion applies to and, for an experiment with random assignment, a cause-and-effect conclusion.
第三部分说明结论类型：结论适用的总体；若是随机分配的实验，可得因果结论。

Breakfast and GPA 早餐与 GPA

A student asks: “For students at our school, is the mean GPA of students who eat breakfast every day greater than the mean GPA of students who do not?” She plans to survey a random sample of 120 students.

aWhat variables guide the data collection?
Variables: whether a student eats breakfast every day (categorical) and GPA (quantitative).
bWhat analysis does the question suggest (test or interval)? Name the parameter and direction.
Analysis: a hypothesis test about the difference in mean GPA (breakfast − no breakfast), direction greater than.
cTo what population can she generalize? Can she conclude that breakfast causes higher GPAs?
Conclusion: she can generalize to all students at the school (random sample) — but no cause-and-effect: this is an observational study, not an experiment with random assignment.

Census vs. Sample 普查与样本

Census普查
A census records information from all items or individuals in a population.
普查：收集总体中所有个体的信息。
Sample survey抽样调查
A sample is a subset of the population from which we actually collect data; a sample survey uses a sample chosen to represent a specific population, and we use it to make an inference about the population.
样本是实际收集数据的总体子集；抽样调查用代表某个总体的样本，对总体做推断。
Why not always take a census? It can be too expensive, too slow, or impossible (testing every light bulb until it burns out!). 普查成本高、耗时长，有时根本做不到。

Observational Study vs. Experiment 观察研究与实验

Experiment实验
An experiment is a study in which a researcher assigns conditions (treatments) to experimental units to explore an investigative question.
实验：研究者把条件（处理）分配给实验单位。
Observational study观察研究
An observational study is a study where treatments are not imposed. The researcher records values of the variables of interest.
观察研究：不施加处理，只记录感兴趣变量的值。

The Language of Experiments 实验的术语

Experimental unit · subjects实验单位 · 受试者
The experimental unit is the unit to which a treatment is assigned. When the units are people, they are called subjects or participants.
实验单位：被分配处理的个体；如果是人，称为受试者。
Factor · levels · treatments因子 · 水平 · 处理
An explanatory variable (factor) has categories (levels) imposed on the units. The levels are the treatments; with more than one factor, each combination of levels is a treatment.
解释变量（因子）的不同类别称为水平，水平就是处理；多个因子时，每种水平组合是一个处理。
Response variable响应变量
A response variable is an outcome measured on each experimental unit after the treatment.
响应变量：施加处理之后，在每个实验单位上测量的结果。

Malaria screening in Kenya 肯尼亚疟疾筛查

Will regularly screening children for the malaria parasite (and treating those who test positive) reduce the proportion of children who develop the disease? Researchers worked with children in 101 schools in Kenya, randomly assigning half of the schools to receive regular screenings and follow-up treatments and the remaining schools to receive no regular screening. Children at all 101 schools were tested for malaria at the end of the study.

Identify the experimental units, the treatments, and the response variable.
Step 1. Experimental units: the 101 schools (treatments were assigned to schools).
Step 2. Treatments: regular screenings and follow-up treatments vs. no regular screening.
Step 3. Response variable: whether each child had malaria at the end of the study (summarized as the proportion of children with malaria at each school).
🔑 Treatments are assigned to schools, not children!

Types of Observational Studies & Confounding 观察研究的类型与混杂

Prospective · retrospective · survey前瞻性 · 回顾性 · 调查
Prospective: units are selected at a point in time and data are gathered then and into the future. Retrospective: units are selected and data from the past are gathered. A survey collects data from humans using a standard set of questions.
前瞻性研究：选定个体后，从现在起向未来追踪收集数据。回顾性研究：收集过去的数据。调查：用标准问题从人那里收集数据。
Confounding variable混杂变量
A confounding variable provides an alternative explanation for the observed relationship between the explanatory and response variables. It must be associated with both the explanatory variable and the response variable.
混杂变量为解释变量与响应变量之间观察到的关系提供了另一种解释；它必须同时与解释变量和响应变量有关联。

📝 Example 5 · 例题★★★★EK 1.10.D.5

Vitamin D and diabetes 维生素 D 与糖尿病

Hundreds of observational studies found that people with higher vitamin D levels in their blood had a lower risk of diabetes.

aExplain why these studies cannot show that vitamin D causes lower diabetes risk. Name a possible confounding variable and explain how it is linked to both variables.
Step 1. Possible confounder: time spent exercising outdoors. People who exercise outdoors get more sun → higher vitamin D; exercise also → lower diabetes risk.
Step 2. So we can’t tell whether vitamin D or exercise is responsible: their effects are confounded.
bResearchers in Norway randomly assigned 500 people with pre-diabetes to take vitamin D or a placebo. After 5 years, about 40% in each group had diabetes. What can we conclude?
The experiment found no difference (≈ 40% vs. ≈ 40%) → vitamin D supplements do not appear to reduce diabetes risk; random assignment removes the confounding.
🔑 Confounder: linked to both explanatory and response.

Generalizing Results 推广结论

Random sample随机样本
A sample is random when all units are selected from the population using a random mechanism (such as a random number generator). Then it is appropriate to generalize to the entire population from which the sample was selected.
如果所有个体都是用随机机制（如随机数生成器）从总体中选出的，样本就是随机的，结论可以推广到整个总体。
Not random非随机
A sample is not random when units are deliberately chosen or volunteer themselves. Then we can generalize only to individuals similar to those in the study.
如果个体是被刻意挑选或自愿参加的，样本就不是随机的，结论只能推广到与研究对象相似的人。

📝 Example 6 · 例题★★★★EK 1.10.E · Skill 2.B

Can we generalize? 能推广吗？

Study A: A fitness app posts an invitation; 2,000 users volunteer to report their weekly exercise hours.

Study B: A school district uses a random number generator to select 150 of its 4,200 high school students and records their weekly exercise hours.

For each study, to what population can the results be generalized? Explain.
Step 1. A: volunteers are not random → generalize only to people similar to these volunteers (active app users who choose to respond) — not to all app users or all adults.
Step 2. B: students were randomly selected from the district’s 4,200 high school students → generalize to all high school students in the district.
🔑 Random selection ↔ generalize to the population.

With or Without Replacement 有放回与无放回

Without replacement无放回
A unit can be selected only once: it is not returned to the population before the next selection.
每个个体最多被选一次：选出后不放回。
With replacement有放回
A unit can be selected more than once: it is returned to the population before the next selection.
个体可能被选多次：选出后放回再选。

Simple Random Sample (SRS) 简单随机样本

SRS简单随机样本
In a simple random sample of size n, every sample of size n has the same chance of being selected. It is the basis for many sampling methods.
简单随机样本：总体中每一个大小为 n 的样本被选中的机会都相同。
How to choose one怎么选
Label each individual 1 to N → Randomize: use a random number generator, numbered slips of paper, or a table of random digits to get n different labels → Select the matching individuals.
编号 1 到 N → 随机化：用随机数生成器、抽签或随机数字表得到 n 个不同的编号 → 选出对应个体。
With a table of random digits: give every label the same number of digits (01–28), read groups of digits left to right, skip numbers not used as labels and repeats. 随机数字表：编号位数相同，跳过无效号与重复号。

Stratified · Cluster · Systematic 分层 · 整群 · 系统抽样

Stratified random sample分层随机抽样
Divide the population into non-overlapping groups (strata) of similar individuals (homogeneous). Take an SRS from each stratum and combine.
把总体分成互不重叠、内部相似的组（层），在每一层中做简单随机抽样，再合并。
Cluster random sample整群随机抽样
Divide the population into clusters that ideally each mirror the population (heterogeneous). Take an SRS of clusters and collect data from all units in the chosen clusters.
把总体分成群，每个群最好像总体的缩影（内部多样）。随机选几个群，调查所选群中的所有个体。
Systematic random sample系统随机抽样
Choose a random starting point, then select every k-th unit (a fixed, periodic interval).
随机选一个起点，然后每隔 k 个选一个。

Name the method 说出抽样方法

A high school has 1,200 students in grades 9–12 and 40 homerooms (each homeroom has students from all grades). The principal wants to survey 80 students about the new schedule. Name each method.

aRandomly select 20 students from each grade.
Stratified random sample (strata = grades).
bRandomly select 3 homerooms and survey every student in them.
Cluster random sample (clusters = homerooms).
cFrom an alphabetical list, randomly pick one of the first 15 names, then every 15th name after that.
Systematic random sample (random start, k = 15; 1,200 ÷ 15 = 80).
dPut all 1,200 names in a random number generator and choose 80.
Simple random sample.
🔑 Strata = SRS within every group; cluster = all units in some groups.

📝 Example 9 · 例题★★★★EK 1.11.B · Skill 2.B

Which method is better — and why? 哪种方法更合适？为什么？

The principal thinks opinions about the new schedule differ a lot by grade (seniors have different classes and free periods).

aExplain why stratifying by grade is a good idea.
Students within a grade likely have similar opinions, and opinions differ between grades → stratifying guarantees each grade is represented and gives a more precise estimate than an SRS of the same size.
bGive one advantage of the cluster sample of homerooms.
Clusters are convenient: surveying 3 homerooms during homeroom time is fast and cheap.
cWhy would stratifying by homeroom be less useful?
Homerooms are mixed (all grades), so they are not homogeneous → stratifying by homeroom would not reduce variability much.
🔑 Strata: similar inside, different between. Clusters: each looks like the population.

Systematic sampling: a caution 系统抽样的注意事项

✅ When it is useful

When there is no list of the population in advance, e.g., an exit poll: survey every 10th voter who leaves the polling place, starting at a random voter among the first 10.

没有总体名单时很方便，例如选举出口民调。

⚠️ When it fails

If the population has a pattern that matches the interval, the sample may not represent the population. Example: apartments listed so that every 10th unit is a corner apartment — a systematic sample with k = 10 might get only corner apartments (or none).

若总体排列的周期与 k 一致，样本可能不具代表性。

Bias in Sampling 抽样偏差

Bias偏差
Bias in a sampling method is a systematic error that results in a statistic being consistently larger or consistently smaller than the parameter it estimates.
偏差：抽样方法中的系统性误差，使统计量总是偏大或总是偏小于要估计的参数。
Nonrandom methods非随机方法
Convenience samples (easy-to-reach individuals) and voluntary response samples (people choose to respond to a general invitation) introduce potential bias because they do not use chance to select individuals.
方便样本（容易接触到的人）和自愿回应样本（人们自己选择是否回应）不使用随机机制，可能带来偏差。

Voluntary Response & Undercoverage 自愿回应偏差与覆盖不足

Voluntary response bias自愿回应偏差
May occur when a sample consists entirely of volunteers (people with strong opinions, often negative, are most likely to respond).
样本全是自愿者时可能出现（立场强烈的人更愿意回应）。
Undercoverage bias覆盖不足偏差
May occur when the sampling method fails to include part of the population, or part of the population is less likely to be selected.
抽样方法漏掉了总体的一部分，或某部分更不容易被选中。
Example: a radio host asks listeners to call in about raising the speed limit (voluntary response). A survey about alcohol use handed out only in on-campus dorms leaves out off-campus students (undercoverage). 电台热线 → 自愿回应；只在宿舍发问卷 → 覆盖不足。

Nonresponse & Response Bias 无回应偏差与回应偏差

Nonresponse bias无回应偏差
May occur when individuals chosen for the sample can’t be contacted or refuse to respond, and they differ from respondents in important ways.
被选中的人联系不上或拒绝回答，且他们与回答者有重要差异。
Response bias回应偏差
May occur when responses tend to differ from the true value in one direction — e.g., leading or confusing question wording, or self-reported answers.
回答系统性地偏离真实值，例如引导性或令人困惑的措辞、自我报告。
Example: in a fitness survey, people may overstate how much they exercise because it is socially desirable (response bias from self-report). 社会期许效应：自我报告的运动量偏高。

Voluntary response ≠ nonresponse 自愿回应 ≠ 无回应

Voluntary response 自愿回应Nonresponse 无回应
Who decides to be in the sample?The people — they answer a general invitationThe researcher chose them (often randomly) …
What goes wrong?Only people with strong opinions join… but some chosen people don’t respond

Name the bias — and its direction 说出偏差类型与方向

aA city wants to know how residents feel about public transit, but only surveys people at bus stops.
Undercoverage (also convenience): car drivers are left out → support for transit is likely overestimated.
bA news website asks: “Do you support the new tax? Click to vote!”
Voluntary response: people with strong (often negative) feelings vote → opposition is likely overestimated.
cA random sample of employees gets an engagement survey by email; many disengaged employees ignore it.
Nonresponse: those who don’t reply are less engaged → engagement is likely overestimated.
dA survey asks students: “How many hours do you study per week?” (Answers are given in front of their teacher.)
Response bias (self-report, social desirability): students may overstate study time → the mean is likely overestimated.
🔑 Type + direction + why.

📝 Example 11 · 例题★★★★EK 1.12.A.5

Question wording 问题措辞

Two versions of a survey question:

V1“How do you feel about the current tax rate?”
V2“Don’t you agree that the current tax rate is too high and hurts working families?”
Which version will likely give a higher proportion saying the tax rate is too high? What kind of bias is this?
Step 1. V2 is a leading question: “Don’t you agree…” and “hurts working families” push people toward “too high”.
Step 2. This is response bias due to question wording: the proportion saying “too high” would likely be larger than the true proportion.
🔑 Leading questions push respondents toward an answer.
💡 Good questions are neutral, simple, and clear.

Identify the sampling method

To estimate the average number of books students read in a summer, a librarian randomly selects 6 of the school’s 50 English classes and surveys every student in those classes. This is
  • (A) a simple random sample
  • (B) a stratified random sample
  • (C) a cluster random sample
  • (D) a systematic random sample
  • (E) a convenience sample

Classes are the groups; an SRS of classes is chosen; all students in them are surveyed.

That is a cluster random sample → (C).

📝 AP Practice 13 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 1.12.A.4

Which bias?

A researcher selects a random sample of 1,000 adults from voter records and mails each a survey about local schools. Only 230 return the survey. The most likely source of bias is
  • (A) voluntary response bias
  • (B) undercoverage
  • (C) nonresponse bias
  • (D) response bias from question wording
  • (E) no bias, because the sample was random

The adults were randomly selected; 770 of them didn’t return the survey.

Selected-but-didn’t-respond → nonresponse bias → (C).

📝 AP Practice 14 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 1.10.E.3–4

Generalizing from volunteers

A psychologist recruits 60 volunteers from her university to test a memory technique. Volunteers who used the technique remembered more words, on average. To which group can the results be generalized?
  • (A) All adults
  • (B) All university students in the country
  • (C) All students at her university
  • (D) Only people similar to the 60 volunteers
  • (E) The results cannot be used at all

The units were volunteers → not a random sample from any larger population.

We can generalize only to individuals similar to those in the study → (D).

📝 AP Practice 15 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 1.10.D

Observational study or experiment?

A study followed 5,000 adults for 10 years and found that those who drank coffee daily had a lower rate of heart disease. Which statement is correct?
  • (A) This is an experiment, so coffee causes lower heart disease.
  • (B) This is a prospective observational study; a confounding variable such as exercise could explain the association.
  • (C) This is a retrospective study, so no conclusion is possible.
  • (D) This is a census of all coffee drinkers.
  • (E) This is an experiment because the researchers recorded heart disease.

Researchers did not assign coffee → observational; following people forward → prospective.

A variable linked to both (e.g., exercise, smoking, income) could explain the association → no cause-and-effect → (B).

📝 AP Practice 16 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 1.11.A.6

Systematic sampling pitfall

A factory produces items on 4 machines in rotation (machine 1, 2, 3, 4, 1, 2, …). An inspector checks every 8th item, starting at a random item among the first 8. Why might this sample be unrepresentative?
  • (A) It is not random.
  • (B) Every selected item comes from the same machine.
  • (C) The sample is too large.
  • (D) It is a cluster sample.
  • (E) Systematic samples always have nonresponse.

Items cycle with period 4; every 8th item lands on the same machine every time.

If that machine differs from the others, the sample is biased → (B).

📝 Exit ticket · 例题★★★★1.10–1.12

Exit ticket 🎟️ 离堂小测

A principal wants to estimate the proportion of the school’s 1,800 students (450 in each of grades 9–12) who support a later start time.

1Describe how to select a stratified random sample of 100 students. Why is it a good choice?
Label the students in each grade 1–450; use a random number generator to pick 25 different labels in each grade; combine to form 100. Good because opinions likely differ by grade (seniors vs. freshmen), so each grade is represented → more precise.
2Instead, she posts an online poll on the school website. Name the bias and its likely direction.
Voluntary response bias: students with strong opinions (likely those who want a later start) respond → the proportion is likely overestimated.
3To whom could the stratified-sample results be generalized?
To all 1,800 students at the school, because they were randomly selected.
🔑 Strata = grades.
