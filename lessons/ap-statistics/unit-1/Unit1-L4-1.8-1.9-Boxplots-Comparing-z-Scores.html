Five-Number Summary & Boxplot 五数概括与箱线图

Five-number summary五数概括
The five-number summary is the minimum, Q₁, median, Q₃, and maximum.
五数概括：最小值、Q₁、中位数、Q₃、最大值。
Boxplot箱线图
A boxplot is a graph of the five-number summary. The box shows the middle 50% of the data, with a line at the median and ends at the quartiles. Whiskers (each about 25% of the data) extend from Q₁ to the minimum and from Q₃ to the maximum. If there are outliers, the whiskers extend to the most extreme values that are not outliers, and outliers are marked with an asterisk (✱) or other symbol.
箱线图是五数概括的图形。箱子表示中间 50% 的数据，中间一条线是中位数，两端是四分位数。须（各约 25% 的数据）从 Q₁ 延伸到最小值、从 Q₃ 延伸到最大值。若有离群值，须只延伸到最极端的非离群值，离群值用 ✱ 等符号单独标出。

How to make a boxplot 如何画箱线图

① Five-number summary · min, Q₁, median, Q₃, max 求五数概括
② Outliers · use the 1.5 × IQR rule 用 1.5×IQR 判断离群值
③ Axis · draw, label (variable + units), and scale 画数轴、标名称、标刻度
④ Box · from Q₁ to Q₃, line at the median Q₁ 到 Q₃ 画箱，中位数画线
⑤ Whiskers · to the most extreme values that are not outliers 须延伸到最远的非离群值
⑥ Outliers · mark each separately with ✱ 离群值单独标出
⚠️ Common mistake: drawing the whisker all the way to an outlier. The whisker stops at the largest (or smallest) value that is not an outlier. 常见错误：须画到离群值。
⚠️ Boxplots do not show individual values, so they hide gaps, clusters, and peaks. A longer section does not mean more data — each section holds about 25%. 箱线图看不到间隙、簇和峰；更长的部分并不代表更多数据。

Boxplot of travel times 画通勤时间的箱线图

Five-number summary of the 20 travel times: 5, 15, 22.5, 42.5, 85 (minutes).
Sorted data: 5 10 10 15 15 15 15 20 20 20 25 30 30 40 40 45 60 60 65 85

aIdentify any outliers.
Fences: 15 − 41.25 = −26.25 and 42.5 + 41.25 = 83.75 → 85 is an outlier.
bMake a boxplot. Where does the right whisker end?
Step 1. Draw and scale the axis “Travel time (minutes)”; draw the box from 15 to 42.5 with a line at 22.5.
Step 2. Left whisker to the min, 5. Right whisker to 65 — the largest value that is not an outlier.
Step 3. Mark 85 with ✱.
🔑 IQR = 42.5 − 15 = 27.5.

Mean, Median, and Shape 均值、中位数与形状

Key idea核心
If a distribution is relatively symmetric, the mean and median are relatively close. If it is skewed right, the mean is usually larger than the median. If it is skewed left, the mean is usually smaller than the median.
分布近似对称：均值与中位数接近。右偏：均值通常大于中位数。左偏：均值通常小于中位数。

📝 Example 4 · 例题★★★★EK 1.8.B.1

What shape? 是什么形状？

For each data set, predict the shape from the mean and median.

aSalaries of NBA players: mean $9.7 million, median $4.3 million.
Mean ≫ median → probably skewed right (a few huge salaries).
bScores on an easy quiz: mean 71, median 78.
Mean < median → probably skewed left (a few very low scores).
cHeights of adult women: mean 162.3 cm, median 162.1 cm.
Mean ≈ median → roughly symmetric.
🔑 Mean is pulled toward the long tail.
💡 “Usually” — this is a clue, not a proof. Always look at a graph if you can!

Comparing Distributions 比较分布

Which graph?用什么图
Histograms, back-to-back stemplots, and dotplots can compare center, variability, shape, outliers, clusters, or gaps. Boxplots can compare center, variability, outliers, and skewness (or symmetry). Comparisons can include any numerical summaries (mean, SD, …).
直方图、背靠背茎叶图、点图可比较中心、变异、形状、离群值、簇和间隙；箱线图可比较中心、变异、离群值和偏态（或对称）。比较中可以使用任何数值概括（均值、标准差等）。
How to compare怎么比较
Address SOCS for each feature, use comparative language (“greater than”, “less than”, “about the same as”), give numbers, and write in context.
对每个特征（SOCS）都要用比较性语言，给出数字，并结合语境。

📝 Example 6 · 例题★★★★EK 1.9.C.1 · Skill 4.B

Sleep and reaction time 睡眠与反应时间

Reaction times (milliseconds) of 24 students: 12 who slept less than 6 hours and 12 who slept at least 8 hours.

aClaim 1: “Every student who slept at least 8 hours was faster than every student who slept less than 6 hours.” Supported?
Claim 1: not supported. The slowest 8-hour sleeper (330 ms) was slower than the fastest short sleeper (262 ms) — the distributions overlap.
bClaim 2: “About 75% of the students who slept at least 8 hours were faster than the median of the short-sleep group.” Supported?
Step 1. Short-sleep median ≈ 303 ms. Q₃ for the 8-hour group is about 280 ms < 303.
Step 2. So at least 75% of the 8-hour sleepers were faster than 303 ms → Claim 2 is supported (in fact 11 of 12).
🔑 Faster = smaller reaction time.

Standardized Scores (z-Scores) 标准分数（z 分数）

z-scorez 分数
A standardized score (z-score) measures how many standard deviations a data value falls above or below the mean. Positive z → above the mean; negative z → below the mean.
标准分数（z 分数）表示一个数据比均值高（或低）多少个标准差。z 为正→高于均值；z 为负→低于均值。
Unknown μ, σ未知参数时
When the population mean and standard deviation are unknown, the sample mean x̄ and standard deviation s may be used.
总体均值和标准差未知时，可以用样本均值 x̄ 与样本标准差 s。
z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\qquad\text{or}\qquad z=\frac{x-\bar{x}}{s}

📝 Example 7 · 例题★★★★EK 1.9.D.2

Jenny’s statistics test Jenny 的统计考试

Here are the scores of all 25 students in Mr. Pryor’s statistics class on their first test. Jenny scored 86.

79 81 80 77 73 83 74 93 78 80 75 67 73 77 83 86 90 79 85 83 89 84 82 77 72

The class mean is 80 and the standard deviation is 6.07.

aFind and interpret Jenny’s z-score.
Step 1. z = (86 − 80) ÷ 6.07 ≈ 0.99.
Step 2. Jenny’s score is about 1 standard deviation above the mean score of the class.
bWhat is Jenny’s percentile?
22 of the 25 scores are ≤ 86 → 22/25 = 88% → Jenny is at the 88th percentile.
🔑 x = 86, μ = 80, σ = 6.07 (the whole class is the population).

📝 Example 8 · 例题★★★★EK 1.9.E.1 · Skill 4.C

Stats or chemistry? 统计还是化学考得更好？

The next day, Jenny earned 82 on Mr. Goldstone’s chemistry test. At first she was disappointed. Then Mr. Goldstone said the distribution of scores was fairly symmetric with a mean of 76 and a standard deviation of 4.

On which test did Jenny perform better relative to her class? Justify your answer.
Step 1. Statistics: z = (86 − 80) ÷ 6.07 ≈ 0.99.
Step 2. Chemistry: z = (82 − 76) ÷ 4 = 1.5.
Step 3. 1.5 > 0.99 → Jenny did better relative to the class on the chemistry test: 1.5 SDs above the mean vs. about 1 SD above.
🔑 Compare z-scores — relative position.
💡 A raw score of 82 can be “better” than 86 — z-scores put different scales on the same footing.

📝 Example 9 · 例题★★★★EK 1.9.D.2

Working backward 反推原始分数

On a test with mean 72 and standard deviation 8:

aMarco’s z-score is −1.5. What was his score?
x = 72 + (−1.5)(8) = 72 − 12 = 60.
bLin scored 92. What is her z-score? Is her score unusual by the 2-SD rule?
Step 1. z = (92 − 72) ÷ 8 = 2.5.
Step 2. |z| = 2.5 > 2 → Lin’s score is more than 2 SDs above the mean → an outlier by the 2-SD rule.
🔑 x = μ + z·σ.
💡 The 2-SD outlier rule from Lesson 3 is the same as |z| > 2.

Parallel boxplots

The boxplots summarize quiz scores in two classes. Which statement must be true?
  • (A) Class A has the higher median.
  • (B) Class A has the larger IQR.
  • (C) Class B has the larger range.
  • (D) More students in B than in A scored above 72.

📝 AP Practice 12 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 1.9.D.2

Interpret a z-score

Ahmed’s z-score for his height among students in his school is −1.2. Which is the best interpretation?
  • (A) Ahmed is 1.2 inches shorter than the mean height.
  • (B) Ahmed’s height is 1.2 standard deviations below the mean height.
  • (C) Ahmed is shorter than 12% of the students.
  • (D) Ahmed’s height is 1.2 standard deviations above the mean height.
  • (E) Ahmed’s height is unusual (an outlier).

Negative z → below the mean; 1.2 is measured in standard deviations, not inches → (B).

|−1.2| < 2 → not an outlier by the 2-SD rule.

📝 AP Practice 14 · AP 选择题★★★★EK 1.7.C.1 · TPS 2.1

Transformation review

Australian students guessed the width of their classroom (meters): mean 16.02, SD 7.14. The teacher defines error = guess − 13, then converts the errors to feet (× 3.28). What are the mean and SD of the errors in feet?
  • (A) Mean 9.91 ft, SD 7.14 ft
  • (B) Mean 52.55 ft, SD 23.42 ft
  • (C) Mean 9.91 ft, SD 23.42 ft
  • (D) Mean 3.02 ft, SD 23.42 ft
  • (E) Mean 9.91 ft, SD 10.40 ft

Mean: (16.02 − 13) × 3.28 = 3.02 × 3.28 ≈ 9.91 ft.

SD: subtracting doesn’t change spread; × 3.28 → 7.14 × 3.28 ≈ 23.42 ft → (C).

Exit ticket 🎟️ 离堂小测

Hours of exercise last week for 11 students:

0 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 10 22
1Find the five-number summary, check for outliers, and sketch a boxplot.
Five-number summary: 0, 3, 6, 9, 22. IQR = 6; high fence = 9 + 9 = 18 → 22 is an outlier; the right whisker ends at 10.
2Ana scored 680 on the SAT Math (mean 520, SD 115). Ben scored 28 on the ACT Math (mean 20.7, SD 5.5). Who did better relative to other test-takers?
z_{Ana} = (680 − 520)/115 ≈ 1.39; z_{Ben} = (28 − 20.7)/5.5 ≈ 1.33 → Ana did slightly better relative to other test-takers.
🔑 n = 11 → median is the 6th value; leave it out of the halves.
